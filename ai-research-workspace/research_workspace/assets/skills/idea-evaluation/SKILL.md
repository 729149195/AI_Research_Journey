# idea-evaluation

## Inputs

Read workspace/templates/idea-evaluation.original.md first, then the author's filled workspace/research/idea-evaluation.md, current research state and actual sources/results. `idea_evluation.md` is a spelling-compatible entry. The source PPT is a visualization-oriented research-planning template, not a scored funding rubric.

## Workflow

1. Preserve the source's ten parts and distinguish them from added Workspace fields: Introduction background/problem; Introduction methods/features; Contributions; Related Work; Your Methods; Initial Results; Expected Results; Discussions; Expected Figures; Remaining Tasks.
2. Ask what concrete problem matters to which task/user/system, what method is proposed, and what benefit would be demonstrated. Keep the source's problem–method–benefits structure. Do not claim novelty or importance without research-specific support.
3. Separate compared work from work the method builds on. Record actual author/year/venue/locator, substantive limitations in context, borrowed components and fair baselines. Unknown literature is a search task, not an invented citation.
4. For visualization research, retain the source's 2–4 concrete visual encoding/layout/interaction methods. Explain mappings, analytical tasks, procedures, cues/interactions, design rationale and alternatives. Do not flatten this into a generic startup Idea template. For other domains, explicitly mark a justified adaptation rather than silently rewriting the source.
5. Keep observed initial results separate from expected results. An absent experiment remains absent. Plan software/platform, each figure's data/effect/purpose, fair comparisons and applicable case study/user study/expert feedback.
6. Examine limitations, transferability, unresolved major problems, workload and milestones. Add explicit disconfirmation criteria, risks and a next experiment only as Workspace extensions.
7. Offer two or three genuinely different feasible research options where useful. Compare question, contribution, evidence requirement, design, cost, risks and what result would change the decision. No fabricated numerical scoring rubric or universal winner. Let the author choose and save the rationale as a non-evidentiary decision.
8. Convert approved direction into question/hypothesis/argument/Claim/method/Figure plans and impact tasks. Draft state first; confirmation and source verification are separate human steps. Revisit the worksheet after meaningful new evidence or reviewer feedback.

## Outputs

A filled or critiqued ten-part worksheet, known/unknown/evidence matrix, bounded contribution statement, alternative options, evaluation/figure plan and reviewable state proposals using base_fingerprint. Clearly label all added Workspace fields and provisional judgments.

## Boundaries

Do not invent an original slide, source recommendation, scoring threshold, experiment, novelty claim or author decision. Do not publish the binary PPT. The original template's rights remain with its rights holder; this repository does not assert a new license over the supplied text.

## Evaluation

With no initial results, keep that section honestly empty and propose a test. Preserve encoding/task/design-rationale questions. A requested stronger contribution must be tied to verifiable benefits. A reviewer-driven direction change must update the broader argument and evidence requirements.
